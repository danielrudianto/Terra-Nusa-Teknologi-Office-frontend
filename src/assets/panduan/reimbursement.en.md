# Reimbursement

Claiming back money that someone paid out of pocket first.

Reimbursement is used when a person pays for something the office needs with
their own money and asks to be repaid. If the payment came straight from a
company account, it isn't a reimbursement — record it as an Expense.

## Filling in a claim

The form has three sections on one page.

### Details

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Date | Date of the claim |
| Due date | When the repayment is expected to clear |
| Expense type | Same list as the Expenses menu |
| Project name | The project bearing the cost |

### Account & payment

Bank name, account number, account name, and payment method (Bank Transfer,
Virtual Account, or Cheque).

The account entered here is the **account receiving the repayment** — the
account of the person who paid out of pocket, not a company account.

### Reimbursement items

Add one row per expense, each with a date, description, and amount. The total
is calculated automatically from all rows.

Split by receipt; don't merge them into one large row. A single row covering
five receipts is hard to check at approval time and hard to trace if there's a
discrepancy.

## The approval flow

A claim can't be paid straight away. The order is:

1. The claim is created
2. **Approve reimbursement** — only for those with approval rights
3. **Create payment** — only becomes available after approval

The **Create payment** menu stays disabled while the claim is unapproved,
already paid, or deleted. The **Approve** menu is disabled once it has been
approved.

The separation is deliberate: whoever claims, whoever approves, and whoever
prepares the money are not the same person.

## After approval

Click a row to see the item details, payment history, and destination account.

**Reprint reimbursement** is available if the document needs printing again —
there's no need to create a new claim.
