# Employees

Recording who works here, and keeping that record correct.

Employee data drives things with real consequences — salary slips, the
emergency contact reached when something happens on site, the number of
dependants behind a tax calculation. Wrong or stale data here is not merely
untidy.

## Three different things

These are often confused, though they serve different purposes.

| | What | When it is filled |
|---|---|---|
| **Employee** | Name, ID number, position, employment status | When someone joins |
| **Personal profile** | Full details: ID card, family, education, bank | Once, after joining |
| **Data update** | Reviewing data that already exists | Any time, at least yearly |

The Employees list shows only the first. The other two open from the three-dot
menu on the row.

## Personal profile

Filled **once**, when a new employee joins.

The **Personal profile** menu appears only while no profile exists. Once
filled, it disappears and is replaced by **Data update**.

That is deliberate. With both open, two paths write the same columns, and
editing the profile directly leaves no trace of who changed what. Through an
update, every change is recorded.

### What to watch when filling it in

**Blood type may be unknown.** Turn on the toggle beside the field. Some people
genuinely never learn it, and a guess on medical data is more dangerous than a
blank.

**A GPA does not always exist.** Secondary schools do not issue one, and some
higher institutions do not either. The toggle beside the field marks that —
which means something different from leaving it empty, which means not yet
filled.

**Family birth dates are picked from a calendar**, not typed. Freely typed
dates produce several different shapes in one column, and none of them can be
sorted.

**Bank names are picked from a list.** Banks outside the list can still be
typed. The point is consistent spelling: "BCA" and "Bank Central Asia" in the
same column make any per-bank summary untrustworthy.

## Data update

Can be done **any time**, without waiting for a period to open.

Every save creates a new record rather than overwriting the old one. Previous
addresses, earlier dependant counts, and when each of those applied all remain.
What applies is the most recent one.

It covers six sections: family and dependants, address and contact, emergency
contact, medical history, training and certification, and willingness to be
assigned. Nothing is mandatory — it can be saved partly and continued later.

### The one-year rule

If someone's data has not been updated for more than **twelve months**, their
name appears in the **Agenda** as needing confirmation. It appears **thirty
days before** the due date, not on the day itself: gathering employee data
means reaching the person and often waiting for them to return from site.

**Any** update counts — filling in the personal profile also stops the clock.
Both are equally a review of the data.

### Saving again without changing anything

That is valid, and in fact expected.

What is confirmed is not that the data changed, but that it is **still
correct**. That is why the button reads "Confirm & save" rather than merely
"Save" — so nobody hesitates to press it when they have changed nothing.

The form header shows when it was last updated. Anything past a year is given a
different colour — not red and not an exclamation mark, because data ageing is
perfectly normal.

## Employment status

An employee who leaves is marked through the **Employment status** menu, by
entering their last working date. They are not deleted.

Deleting would break the salary slips, purchase orders, and other records that
refer to their name. Someone who has left is also no longer chased for data
updates and no longer appears as a birthday in the Agenda.

## Who is allowed

Personal profiles and data updates can only be opened by the **HR division**
and the owner. This limit cannot be bypassed with a higher level: the contents
are personal data that other divisions do not need.
