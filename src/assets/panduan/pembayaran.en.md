# Payments

Approving and tracing outgoing payments.

This menu is not where payments are created. Payments are created from their
source document — Purchase, Expense, Reimbursement, Salary Slip, or Loan — and
then appear here for approval.

## Where payments come from

| Document type | Created from |
|---|---|
| Purchase | Purchases menu, "Create payment" |
| Expense | Expenses menu |
| Reimbursement | Reimbursement menu, after approval |
| Salary | Salary Slip menu |
| Loan | Loans menu |

So if a payment doesn't appear, the thing to check is its source document — not
this menu.

## Approving

Each row has an **Approve** and a **Reject** menu. The list can be filtered
between approved and not yet approved.

Before approving, open **View payment details** and check the account number
against the original document. Approval is the last point before money moves;
after that, nobody checks again.

## Whoever creates it cannot approve it

A payment you created yourself **cannot be approved by you**. The button is
disabled, and the reason appears on hover: *"A payment you created yourself
must be approved by another user."*

This is deliberate, not a malfunction. One person able to both create and
approve a payment means there is no check at all on company spending. If your
payment is urgent, ask an authorised colleague to approve it — don't look for a
way around it.

## Rejecting

A payment with wrong data is better **rejected** than left hanging. The source
document still exists, and a new payment can be created with correct data.

A list full of dangling payments makes the ones genuinely awaiting approval
hard to find.

## Payment history

The **Payment history** menu shows payments that have gone through, complete
with their document context: project, supplier, invoice number, receipt, tax
invoice, DPP, and VAT.

Useful for two things: answering a supplier asking "has my invoice been paid",
and matching outgoings against the bank statement.

## Payment calendar

The **Calendar** menu shows the schedule and balance per day — outgoings,
incoming money, and balance. Useful for seeing which days are heavy before
promising a payment date to a supplier.
